And if the cardiac activity is visible? That's excellent news, a real milestone. But let's talk numbers, honestly. What's your guess: out of ten IVF pregnancies where we've seen the heartbeat, how many keep going?

These are broad statistical benchmarks, not your individual situation. An older American study, from a single center, followed more than two thousand IVF pregnancies with cardiac activity seen at around seven weeks. Under 35, more than 9 in 10 kept developing. Between 35 and 39, a little under 9 in 10. And from 40 on, a little under 8 in 10. A Chinese study published in 2016 found a similar picture. In more than two thousand five hundred IVF pregnancies where the heartbeat had been seen, about 9 in 10 got past the first trimester. And there too, the risk went up with age.

Some of these numbers are a reminder to stay cautious. But they describe groups, not your personal story. Seeing that heartbeat is a major milestone. Not a promise carved in stone, but a huge step forward.

Now, safety. Straight talk.

A pregnancy can sometimes implant outside the uterus, most often in a fallopian tube: that's an ectopic pregnancy. In large IVF statistics, it affects about 1 to 2 pregnancies in 100, and even fewer after transferring a single frozen blastocyst. The risk is higher if your tubes have been damaged or operated on, or if you've had an ectopic pregnancy before. The early scan is there precisely to check where the pregnancy implanted.

And much more rarely, there can be two pregnancies at the same time: one in the uterus, another outside it. That's called a heterotopic pregnancy. With IVF, it's a few cases per thousand, or fewer. That's why the doctor also checks both sides of the uterus.

So, even after a reassuring scan that morning, some signs should never wait for the next appointment. Sharp pain in the lower belly, especially on one side. Unusual pain at the tip of the shoulder. Feeling faint, or passing out. Or heavy bleeding.

In those cases, you don't ask the internet. You go straight to the emergency department, or call 15 in France, one-one-two anywhere in Europe, or your local emergency number.

For light bleeding or brown discharge, contact your center. For persistent pain, even moderate, call your team the same day as well.

And your support medications, progesterone included: keep taking them exactly as prescribed, until the exact date your center gave you. Even after a beautiful image, you never stop a treatment on your own.

A scan at around five weeks can sometimes help us locate the pregnancy. But if the question is "Is the embryo developing well?", it's likely to leave us with an incomplete answer.
