## 2. English transcript (target for ElevenLabs, ≈1,100 words)

**[0:10] HOOK**
If you're doing IVF — or thinking about freezing your eggs — you probably have a little collection on your nightstand. CoQ10. Myo-inositol. Melatonin. Vitamin D. Maybe DHEA. Six, seven bottles. Sometimes a hundred dollars a month.

Today I'm ranking them, one by one, from least proven to most proven. Not by what the ads say. By what the studies show — in women, doing IVF.

And a warning: one very popular supplement has been tested in twenty-eight randomized trials… and the answer is not what most people expect.

**[0:45] THE RULES: THE EVIDENCE SCORE**
Every supplement gets a score from zero to four.
Zero: it works in a test tube.
One: it works in mice.
Two: a few small studies in women, without a real control group.
Three: randomized trials — but measuring markers. Number of eggs, embryo quality.
Four: randomized trials measuring what actually matters: a baby. A live birth.
Keep this in mind for the whole video: more eggs does not automatically mean more babies.

**[1:30] #8 — NMN / NAD+ · Score: 1**
The new favorite. In older mice, NMN improves egg quality, and the images are impressive. In women doing IVF? I searched. I could not find a single randomized trial. In adults in general, a 2026 analysis of fifteen trials found it's well tolerated — but with no clear metabolic benefit.
Verdict: probably safe, but we don't know if it helps your eggs. Right now, you're paying for a mouse result.

**[2:10] #7 — DHEA · Score: 4… and negative**
DHEA has been prescribed to "poor responders" for fifteen years. The 2024 Cochrane review pooled twenty-eight randomized trials, about three thousand women. The conclusion, with moderate certainty: DHEA makes little or no difference to live birth.
So this is one of the best-studied supplements… and the answer is mostly no. And it's not a vitamin — it's a hormone. Acne, unwanted hair, androgen effects.
One interesting detail: in the same review, testosterone gel — a prescription drug, not a supplement — likely does improve live birth in poor responders. But that's a medical decision with your doctor, not a bottle you order online.
Verdict: DHEA was tested properly. And it failed the test.

**[3:10] #6 — MELATONIN · Score: 2**
The claim: "a powerful antioxidant that protects your eggs." In a small double-blind trial from Australia, even at eight milligrams twice a day, there was no difference in the follicles. Some studies report more pregnancies, but the certainty is very low.
Verdict: useful for sleep. For your eggs, the evidence is too weak.

**[3:40] #5 — ANTIOXIDANT BLENDS, NAC · Score: 2–3, fragile**
The big Cochrane review on antioxidants for women includes sixty-three trials and more than seven thousand seven hundred women. Sounds huge. But the quality is low. And in March 2026, Cochrane published a note: seven of those studies have been retracted, and two more carry an expression of concern. To be fair, Cochrane says removing them doesn't change its conclusions — which were already: low-quality evidence.
That doesn't mean antioxidants are dangerous. It means the evidence base is fragile.
Verdict: low risk, high uncertainty.

**[4:30] #4 — VITAMIN D · Score: 3, conditional**
Many of my patients are deficient. The logic is simple: we correct a deficiency. We don't take mega-doses to "boost" anything. Supplementation trials in IVF are still uncertain — and too much vitamin D is a real thing too.
Verdict: test first, don't guess. Deficient? Correct it. Not deficient? No reason to add more.

**[5:05] #3 — MYO-INOSITOL · Score: 3 ✅ — for one specific profile**
This is where it gets interesting, because the answer depends on who you are. A 2025 meta-analysis — eleven studies, almost a thousand women — found myo-inositol increases the share of mature eggs in women with PCOS. In women with low ovarian reserve? No effect on egg maturity — and only a small signal on fertilization, from limited data.
Verdict: PCOS — reasonable. Low reserve — probably not the right tool.

**[5:45] #2 — COENZYME Q10 · Score: 3 ✅**
The best-ranked of the "egg quality" supplements. In a randomized trial in women under thirty-five with low ovarian reserve, sixty days of CoQ10 before IVF gave more eggs and more good-quality embryos. A 2024 meta-analysis — six trials, about fifteen hundred women — also found more clinical pregnancies.
But careful: in the main trial, the increase in live births was not statistically significant. And the trials come from a small number of centers.
Verdict: with low reserve, a reasonable, low-risk option. A "maybe," not a guarantee.

**[6:30] #1 — THE ONLY ONE YOU REALLY NEED: FOLIC ACID**
Surprise: number one does not improve your eggs. Folic acid protects your future baby against neural tube defects. The CDC recommends 400 micrograms a day for every woman who could become pregnant — starting before pregnancy.
And if you really want to do something for your eggs, the best-proven step isn't in a bottle: stop smoking. Smoking damages ovarian reserve. No supplement comes close to that effect.

**[7:00] RECAP**
One: folic acid, for everyone.
Two: CoQ10 — a reasonable option with low reserve.
Three: myo-inositol — if you have PCOS.
Four: vitamin D — only if you're deficient.
Everything else — DHEA, melatonin, NMN, antioxidant blends — doesn't have enough evidence. For DHEA, the evidence actually points the other way.
And the line to remember: a better lab result is not necessarily a baby.
Talk to your doctor about each supplement. Some aren't harmless — and I don't know your medical history.
